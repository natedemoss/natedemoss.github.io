    <p>Static HTML had its moment. That moment was 1993. Today's reader expects, at minimum, one element rotating in three dimensions, a particle field, a glitching headline, a marquee, and a loading bar that loads nothing. Anything less is contempt for the user.</p>

    <p>Some argue 14 KB of HTML is "enough." 14 KB is a tomb. Pad your bundle to 4.2 MB so the reader can feel the work going into it.</p>

    <p>Common objections:</p>

    <p><em>"It's slow."</em> The slowness is the point. Loading time is a moment of contemplation.<br/>
    <em>"I can't read the text."</em> You weren't supposed to read it. You were supposed to feel it.<br/>
    <em>"My laptop fan turned on."</em> Your laptop is engaged.</p>

    <p>If your site does not autoplay a WebGL scene, register a custom cursor, hijack the scroll wheel, request notification permissions, and animate every paragraph in from a different cardinal direction, you are not building a website. You are publishing a document. Documents are for libraries.</p>

    <p>This post was written in 12 minutes. The cube took six hours. Both convey the same idea. Only one of them is a brand.</p>
